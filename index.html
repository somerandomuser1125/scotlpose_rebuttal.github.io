1. Additional visualizations on MPI-INF-3DHP

This section presents additional visualizations on the MPI-INF-3DHP dataset. In these examples, the baseline model sometimes achieves slightly better P-MPJPE than SCoTL-pose, but SCoTL-pose obtains better scores on our structure-aware metrics (LSE, BSLE, LLE). Qualitatively, SCoTL-pose produces poses that are closer to the ground truth and more structurally plausible.

We briefly summarize the three structure-aware metrics used in the tables below:

LSE (Limb Symmetry Error). LSE measures left–right asymmetry of corresponding limb pairs (e.g., lower arms, thighs). For a symmetric limb pair \((\mathbf{l}_{i1}, \mathbf{l}_{i2})\) and \((\mathbf{r}_{i1}, \mathbf{r}_{i2})\):

\[ \mathrm{LSE}_i = 100 \cdot \left| \frac{\lVert \mathbf{l}_{i1} - \mathbf{l}_{i2} \rVert - \lVert \mathbf{r}_{i1} - \mathbf{r}_{i2} \rVert} {\left( \lVert \mathbf{l}_{i1} - \mathbf{l}_{i2} \rVert + \lVert \mathbf{r}_{i1} - \mathbf{r}_{i2} \rVert \right) / 2} \right|. \]

BSLE (Body Segment Length Error). BSLE measures the relative length error of each body segment between prediction and ground truth. For a segment with predicted keypoints \(\mathbf{k}_{i1}, \mathbf{k}_{i2}\) and ground truth \(\mathbf{t}_{i1}, \mathbf{t}_{i2}\):

\[ \mathrm{BSLE}_i = 100 \cdot \left| 1 - \frac{\lVert \mathbf{k}_{i2} - \mathbf{k}_{i1} \rVert} {\lVert \mathbf{t}_{i2} - \mathbf{t}_{i1} \rVert} \right|. \]

LLE (Limb Length Error). LLE is BSLE restricted to limb segments, where structural artifacts are most visually apparent. For all three metrics, lower values indicate more structurally consistent poses.

3. Pose-net and Loss-net diagram

The figure below illustrates the overall architecture of the pose-net and the graph-based loss-net.

Given input 2D keypoints, the pose-net lifts them to 3D keypoints. The resulting 3D keypoints are then concatenated with the input 2D keypoints and a joint embedding to form joint-level node features.

These node features are fed into a graph-based loss-net, where attention is biased using a skeleton-derived adjacency that encodes the kinematic structure. A CLS token aggregates global information and the loss-net outputs a single energy scalar for the pose, which acts as a learned structure-preserving loss.

During training, the pose-net and loss-net are updated in an alternating manner, so that the loss-net learns to assign lower energy to structurally plausible poses and higher energy to implausible ones, while the pose-net learns to minimize this energy together with the supervised error.
