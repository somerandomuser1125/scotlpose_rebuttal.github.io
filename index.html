On Human3.6M, even though the benchmark is already highly saturated, the diffusion-based model benefits from leveraging multiple negative samples and still achieves a noticeable improvement, outperforming KTPFormer by 1.2 points on MPJPE. We also observe that, despite KTPFormer itself being near the performance ceiling, incorporating our method still leads to further gains. On MPI-INF-3DHP, our approach consistently improves performance across settings as well. Unfortunately, for KTPFormer on 3DHP we were unable to run experiments, because the official baseline code is not publicly released and there are open reproducibility issues reported in the community that have not yet been addressed in the official repository.

For P-STMO, due to the deadline, both the baseline and the +SCoTL-Pose had not been trained for the full number of epochs and were reported with preliminary hyperparameters. After the submission, we completed training to convergence and performed a more thorough hyperparameter search for both models, and we now report these updated, fully trained P-STMO results in the revised manuscript.

3. Does energy model bring better structure?

Yes of course! We summarize here how the learned energy from the loss-net relates to structural plausibility:

  • Following the loss-net gradient (GBI) improves poses: A few gradient steps on the loss-net energy consistently reduce both P-MPJPE and structural errors (LSE, BSLE, LLE), so the energy gives meaningful guidance, not random regularization.
  • Effect is not just “lower P-MPJPE”: Even when baseline and SCoTL-Pose have matched P-MPJPE, SCoTL-Pose still has lower LSE/BSLE/LLE, meaning the loss-net has learned preferences for more structurally plausible poses beyond P-MPJPE.
  • Energy is aligned with structural metrics: Within fixed P-MPJPE bins, the graph-based loss-net energy is positively correlated with LSE/BSLE/LLE; for LSE, Kendall τ≈0.24–0.26 (~62–63% correct ordering), showing strong sensitivity to symmetry and limb-length consistency.
  • Graph-based > MLP loss-net: Compared to an MLP, the graph-based loss-net has consistently higher correlation (~+0.1 τ) with all structural metrics, indicating that explicitly encoding the skeleton graph makes the energy much better at ranking structural plausibility.

To better understand the behavior of SCoTL-Pose, we examined samples on which SCoTL-Pose performs similarly to, or even worse than, the baseline in terms of P-MPJPE. Somewhat surprisingly, visualization of these cases revealed that SCoTL-pose often produces poses that are structurally more plausible. For the same samples, SCoTL-pose also achieves higher scores under our proposed structural metrics. This analysis reinforces two key points: (i) the loss-net effectively captures structural signals and guides the pose-net toward more structural consistent pose, and (ii) P-MPJPE alone is insufficient to assess structural consistency, underscoring the meaningful role of our structural metrics as a complementary evaluation measure.

4. Cross-dataset evaluation between H36M and 3DHP to examine domain-specific overfitting

To verify that SCoTL-Pose does not overfit to a particular dataset, we conduct cross-dataset experiments. When we train on H36M and evaluate on MPI-INF-3DHP, the baseline achieves 169.75 P-MPJPE, while SCoTL-Pose obtains 163.04. Conversely, when we train on 3DHP and evaluate on H36M, the baseline reaches 187.21 P-MPJPE and SCoTL-Pose improves this to 181.82. Although these cross-dataset results are not fully aligned with our initial expectations and the overall performance decreases under the dataset shift, we regard this degradation as mainly stemming from the backbone pose model and the dataset mismatch, rather than from our loss-net itself. Importantly, in all cross-dataset settings SCoTL-Pose consistently outperforms the baseline, indicating that our loss-net does not simply overfit to a single domain but provides a robust structural prior. We believe that explicitly improving cross-dataset generalization is an interesting direction for future work, and we plan to investigate how the learned loss-net can be further exploited to contribute to better handling of distribution shifts.

5. Pose-net and Loss-net diagram

The figure below illustrates the overall architecture of the pose-net and the graph-based loss-net.

Given input 2D keypoints, the pose-net lifts them to 3D keypoints. The resulting 3D keypoints are then concatenated with the original 2D keypoints and a learned joint-type embedding to form joint-level node features. These node features are fed into a loss-net, for which we experiment with two variants: a simple MLP-based loss-net and a graph-based loss-net.

In particular, the graph-based loss-net explicitly reflects the human skeleton: joints are treated as nodes, edges follow the kinematic connectivity, and a CLS token attends to all joints to aggregate global pose information. The loss-net outputs a single scalar energy for each pose, which serves as a learned structural consistent loss.

During training, the pose-net and loss-net are optimized in an alternating way. In i) of the diagram, we update only the pose-net, minimizing a combination of the supervised loss and the energy predicted by the loss-net. In ii), we freeze the pose-net and update the loss-net so that it learns to assign lower energy to structurally plausible poses and higher energy to implausible ones. This way, the loss-net provides structural guidance to the pose-net, while the pose-net learns to produce poses with both low supervised error and low structural energy.
